5.2 **What we hold about you.** Your Twitch username and display name, your message text and emotes, the timestamp, and whether the message was approved, rejected, or re-aired. **We do not currently store your role or badges (moderator, subscriber, etc.) alongside a message** — that information is used only to render the live chat view in the dashboard and is discarded rather than saved to history.

5.3 **Our role.** For viewer messages we act largely as a **processor** on the streamer's instructions (GDPR Art. 28). This isn't just a policy label: no chat message is captured until a streamer has both connected their Twitch account and been manually approved for access, and the service only ever connects to a channel the account-holder controls or has explicitly invited a moderator into — there is no path by which we attach to a channel without that channel owner's own action. The streamer determines which channel is connected and which messages are broadcast; they are the controller for that decision. We do not use viewer messages for our own purposes, do not profile viewers, and do not sell or share this data.

5.4 **Transparency.** Because we obtained your data from Twitch rather than from you directly, GDPR Art. 14 applies. This section is that notice, and it is backed by a concrete mechanism rather than standing alone: streamers are contractually required to add a Twitch channel panel disclosing StreamCast Pro's use and linking here, within 7 days of their access being approved (Terms §3.7). ## 12. Security

> **Plain English Summary**: OAuth means we never touch your password. Roles are enforced on the server, not just hidden in the interface. Your overlay URL is effectively a password — don't show it on stream. Right now, one specific gap: your Twitch token isn't separately encrypted beyond the database's own default protection.

12.1 Authentication is OAuth-only; no passwords are stored. Role gating is enforced server-side (Firestore security rules), not merely in the client UI.

12.2 Data is encrypted in transit via TLS. Most data at rest is protected only by Firestore's own default platform-level encryption. **Your Twitch OAuth token specifically is additionally encrypted at the application layer (AES-256-GCM) before it ever reaches the database, using a key that exists only on the server and is never sent to any browser** — even someone with direct database read access, or the app's own master-admin account browsing the data through the client, would see only ciphertext, not your token.

12.3 Access to production data is scoped as follows: within the application, exactly one Firebase Auth account (the project owner) has a full read/write bypass over the entire database via server-enforced security rules; every other signed-in user is restricted to their own channel's data, or to a channel that has explicitly invited them as a moderator. [VERIFY — this describes application-level access control only. Anyone holding a Firebase/Google Cloud service-account credential has infrastructure-level access independent of these rules; confirm how many such credentials exist and how they are stored/secured, since that determines the true answer to "who has production data access."]

12.4 **Overlay URLs contain access tokens.** Anyone holding one can push content to your stream. Do not display them on stream or share them publicly. We may rotate a URL we believe is exposed.

12.5 No system is perfectly secure. In the event of a personal data breach we will notify the Datenschutzbehörde within 72 hours where required under Art. 33, and affected individuals without undue delay where Art. 34 applies.
